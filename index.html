Science Policy Canadian science policy, pediatric drug safety, clinical trials, stakeholder engagement, and implementation. +

This course introduced me to the process of developing science policy within the Canadian federal government. Working as part of a policy team, I examined a national health issue and worked through identifying barriers, reviewing evidence, developing solutions, engaging stakeholders, managing risks, and considering how a policy could be implemented and evaluated.

Our policy focus: pediatric drug development and the lack of pediatric-specific clinical research in Canada. Our work examined gaps in pediatric evidence, including challenges with off-label medication use, age-appropriate formulations, recruitment, ethics, safety, and limited investment in pediatric trials.

Selected experiences

  • Completed an environmental scan and literature review to understand the problem and existing approaches.
  • Developed a proposed strategy to increase funding and incentives for pediatric medication research and support more pediatric-specific clinical trials.
  • Examined how better pediatric evidence could improve information about medication dosing, safety, and effectiveness.
  • Developed stakeholder engagement strategies involving patients and families, researchers, pharmaceutical companies, government, clinicians, and other groups.
  • Considered risks such as low participation, recruitment challenges, ethical concerns, and difficulty translating research into practice.

Capstone Project

My capstone follows three milestones, allowing the project to develop from an initial environmental scan into a systematic review and, ultimately, evidence-based calls to action.

Beyond Income: Examining Oral Health Disparities Among Canada's Homeless Population

My capstone project examines oral health disparities among people experiencing homelessness in Canada. The project considers the interconnected social, environmental, clinical, and healthcare-system factors that contribute to these disparities and explores the issue through an interdisciplinary lens.

Capstone Group

Teagan Benson
Benjamin Zhang
Isabella Tullo
Ashima Verma

Milestone 1: Environmental Scan

This milestone focuses on developing an environmental scan around the wicked problem and exploring the issue from multiple perspectives. It brings together background information, interest holders, social and environmental factors, clinical context, patient experience, and relevant research areas.

Overview

Our work explores why oral health disparities among people experiencing homelessness are influenced by much more than income alone. The project considers barriers such as the cost and availability of dental care, housing instability, transportation, continuity of care, competing priorities, stigma, and experiences within the healthcare system.

Key areas explored

  • Financial barriers and gaps in dental coverage
  • Behavioral and social factors, including food insecurity and competing priorities
  • Continuity of care and access to oral hygiene infrastructure
  • Healthcare engagement, stigma, and experiences with dental services
  • Clinical issues including caries, periodontal disease, diagnosis, physiology, and treatment
  • Patient experiences and potential low-barrier, outreach-based approaches to care

The project emphasizes that oral health disparities among people experiencing homelessness are influenced by interacting barriers and therefore require perspectives from multiple interest-holder groups rather than a single solution.
